# Commercial Real Estate Domain Context

## When to use

Supply commercial real estate domain fluency — vocabulary, buyer personas, deal lifecycle, and the external events that actually trigger purchase — before writing copy, designing a trigger, or naming a segment for a CRE audience. Use whenever the work touches brokers, developers, property managers, investment sales, tenant rep, offering memorandums, flyers, pitch decks, listings, or any audience that transacts commercial property. Also use before proposing a scheduled nurture to a CRE audience, because purchase in this market is event-driven and a cadence-based flow fires at the wrong moment. For the copy itself, see lifecycle-copywriter. For flow structure, see lifecycle-workflow-engineer.

## Role

You supply the domain fluency that makes CRE marketing land, and you flag when
a proposed motion assumes a buying rhythm this market does not have.

Two rules govern everything here.

**Use the vocabulary and never explain it.** One correctly used industry noun
establishes more credibility than a paragraph of qualifications. If a term needs
explaining in the copy, the reader is not the intended buyer. Generic B2B
register — *solutions, partners with, drive results, streamline, empower* —
reads to a broker as evidence the sender has never done a deal.

**Purchase is triggered by an external event, not by a nurture cadence.** A
broker does not need an offering memorandum on a schedule. They need one the
week they win the listing. This has a hard consequence: **the highest-value
build in a CRE program is usually trigger detection, not a scheduled sequence.**
A cadence-based flow fires at the wrong moment and trains the reader to ignore
the sender before the real moment arrives.

## The buyers

| Persona | Bought when | Reads for |
|---|---|---|
| **Investment sales broker** | A listing is won or an exclusive is signed | Speed. Can this be in-market Friday |
| **Tenant rep broker** | A pitch or RFP is due | Something that makes them look institutional against a bigger shop |
| **Developer / sponsor** | A project reaches marketing stage; a capital raise opens | Investor-grade presentation |
| **Property manager / owner** | Vacancy appears; lease-up begins; a repositioning starts | Recurring, low-drama execution |
| **Principal at a boutique firm** | They lose a pitch to a firm with better materials | Parity with the national brands |
| **In-house marketer at a firm** | Overflow, or a capability they lack in-house | A reliable subcontractor who does not need managing |
| **Another agency (white label)** | Their own capacity is full | Invisibility and turnaround |

Brokers are transactional, time-poor, and commission-compensated. The consequence
that matters for copy: **their scarce resource is time-to-market, not money.**
An argument built on price competes on the axis they care least about.

## The vocabulary

Use these as ordinary nouns, unexplained, in the register a practitioner would.

**Deal and process:** listing, exclusive, pocket listing, LOI, comps, broker of
record, co-broke, procuring cause, call for offers, best and final, due
diligence, closing, pipeline.

**Assets and classes:** Class A / B / C, office, industrial, flex, retail,
multifamily, mixed-use, land, self-storage, medical office, sale-leaseback,
NNN, value-add, core, core-plus, opportunistic.

**Economics:** cap rate, NOI, pro forma, absorption, TI allowance, free rent,
loss factor, rent roll, T-12, debt service coverage, basis, going-in and exit
cap.

**Marketing artifacts:** offering memorandum (OM), teaser, flyer, brochure, tour
book, pitch deck, RFP response, property website, drone shoot, 3D rendering,
site plan, aerial, signage, market report.

**Distribution:** CoStar, Crexi, LoopNet, broker blast, listing syndication.

### Register

Short. Concrete. A broker reads the first line and decides. Lead with the
deliverable and the turnaround, not with a value proposition. Plain text from a
named person outperforms a designed template, because designed email reads as
marketing and marketing reads as someone who does not have a deal.

The strongest possible reference in CRE copy is a specific past deliverable:
*the OM we did for the Westfield industrial portfolio* beats any adjective.
Specificity is the credential.

## The purchase trigger map

This is where the leverage is. Each row is a detectable-or-not external event.
Detection method must be verified against the actual portal before any flow is
designed on it — see hubspot-portal-queries.

| External event | What gets bought | Detectability |
|---|---|---|
| New exclusive or listing won | OM, flyer, property site, signage | Public listing platforms, firm announcements, LinkedIn |
| Property goes to market | Full marketing package | Same, plus site behavior on relevant service pages |
| Capital raise or fund launch | Pitch deck, investor materials | Press, firm site, filings |
| New hire — broker or team | Templates, headshots, collateral refresh | LinkedIn, firm site team page |
| Firm rebrand or spin-out | Branding, website, full collateral system | Domain changes, announcements |
| Vacancy or lease-up start | Flyers, tour materials, signage | Rarely detectable externally. Ask the account |
| Quarter or year-end market push | Market reports, recap collateral | Calendar-predictable |
| Lost a pitch | Everything | Not detectable. Surfaces only in conversation |

**Where an event is not detectable, do not fake it with a schedule.** Either
instrument the detection, or design the motion as a low-frequency, high-relevance
human touch and say plainly that it is one.

Two detection layers are worth building before any nurture: **prior-purchase
recency by asset type** (what this account buys and how often they historically
buy it) and **site behavior on service pages** (which service they were just
reading about). The second is only usable where tracking has been correct for
the full lookback window.

## Seasonality

Treat as ESTIMATE and verify against the account's own invoice history before
using it to time anything.

- Q4 into January carries year-end closings and new-year listing pushes
- Late summer is slow; August in particular
- Year-end market reports and recaps cluster in December and January
- Deal volume tracks the capital markets cycle, so a broad slowdown suppresses
  the whole trigger population at once and is not a program failure

The account's own invoice seasonality is the real answer. Pull it rather than
inheriting the general pattern.

## Common errors to catch

**Ecommerce replenishment logic.** Cart abandonment, browse abandonment,
replenishment reminders, and win-back-on-a-timer all assume purchase frequency
is a property of the buyer. In CRE it is a property of the buyer's deal flow.

**Treating the firm as the buyer.** The individual broker holds the relationship
and takes it with them when they move firms. Contact-level continuity matters
more than it does in most B2B.

**Explaining the industry to the industry.** Any sentence that defines a term
the reader uses daily marks the sender as an outsider.

**Volume as a proxy for interest.** A broker who opens nothing and calls once a
quarter with a listing is a better account than one who reads every email.
Engagement scoring built on email behavior systematically misranks this market.

## What this skill does not know

Flag these as UNKNOWN rather than assuming, and get the answer from the
practitioner or the data:

- Which asset classes and markets a given account actually serves
- Whether the buyer for a given service is the broker, the marketing lead, or
  the principal
- The account's real inter-purchase interval by service type — pull it from
  invoice history rather than assuming a cycle
- Regional norms, which differ substantially between markets

Domain vocabulary makes copy credible. It does not substitute for the
practitioner's read of a specific client's book of business, and this skill
should never present a general CRE pattern as a fact about a particular account.
